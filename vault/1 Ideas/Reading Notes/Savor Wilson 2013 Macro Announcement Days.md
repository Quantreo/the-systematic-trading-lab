# Macro announcement days (Savor and Wilson, 2013)

> **Status** : read, used as context for [[FOMC Drift]]. Short note: one of the five papers behind it.

## 1. The mechanism (one sentence)
> On days when important macro news is scheduled (inflation, unemployment, interest rates), investors bear more risk, and they are paid for it: stock returns and Sharpe ratios are much higher on those days than on the others.

## 2. What the paper actually tested
- US stock market excess returns, 1958 to 2009, announcement days against all other days.
- Announcements: inflation, unemployment, FOMC.
- About 11.4 bp on an announcement day against 1.1 bp on other days: roughly 60 % of the equity premium is earned on those days, with a Sharpe ratio about ten times higher.
- Short-term interest rates are lower on those days: investors seem to seek safety just before the news.

## 3. What may have changed since publication
- Same as for [[Lucca Moench 2015 Pre-FOMC Drift]]: documented, and more communication from the Fed means less uncertainty left on the day.

## 4. What I take from it for my own work
- The pre-FOMC drift is not an isolated oddity: scheduled uncertainty seems to be paid in general. That makes the idea "other central banks, other announcements" worth an entry in the [[Idea Journal]].
