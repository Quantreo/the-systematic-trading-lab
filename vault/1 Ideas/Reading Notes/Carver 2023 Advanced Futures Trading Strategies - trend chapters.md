# Carver, Advanced Futures Trading Strategies: the trend chapters

> **Status** : read, used for [[Crypto XS Momentum]]. A note on a few chapters of a book, not on the whole book: you take what your strategy needs.

## 1. The mechanism (one sentence)
> Trend following earns because prices keep moving in the direction of recent moves; the book is less about why than about **how** to turn a trend into a position that is sized, combined and traded cheaply.

## 2. What the chapters actually do (strategies 6 to 9)
- **Strategy 6, slow trend, long and short** : one moving-average crossover, long or short.
- **Strategy 7, trend strength** : the position follows how strong the trend is, not just its sign. The forecast is scaled so that its average absolute value is 10, and capped at 20.
- **Strategy 8, fast trend with buffering** : a faster signal, and a buffer so that a small change of forecast does not trigger a trade.
- **Strategy 9, several trend rules** : several speeds averaged, then multiplied by a forecast diversification multiplier (FDM), because averaging correlated signals shrinks them.
- Sizing throughout: each instrument scaled to the same risk (inverse volatility), the whole book to a volatility target.

## 2c. What surprised me
- The work is in the plumbing: scaling, capping, buffering, combining. The signal itself is one line.

## 3. What may have changed since publication
- Written for futures. Crypto perps add funding, a universe that changes every month, and names that die.

## 4. How I use it
- In [[Crypto XS Momentum]]: three speeds averaged with an FDM (strategy 9), forecast scaled to 10 and capped at 20 (strategy 7), a buffer on positions (strategy 8), inverse-vol sizing and a vol target.
- What is **not** from these chapters: the cross-sectional step (demeaning across the universe) and the point-in-time universe.
