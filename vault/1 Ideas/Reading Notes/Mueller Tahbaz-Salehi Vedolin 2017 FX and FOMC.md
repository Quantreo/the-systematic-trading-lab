# Exchange rates and monetary policy uncertainty (Mueller, Tahbaz-Salehi and Vedolin, 2017)

> **Status** : read, used for [[FOMC Drift]]. Short note: one of the five papers behind it, and the one that moved the idea from equities to FX.

## 1. The mechanism (one sentence)
> Being short the US dollar against other currencies earns significantly more on FOMC announcement days, as compensation for monetary policy uncertainty borne by constrained intermediaries.

## 2. What the paper actually tested
- A short-USD, long-other-currencies strategy, on days with and without scheduled FOMC announcements.
- The excess returns are higher for currencies with a higher interest rate differential against the US, increase with uncertainty about monetary policy, and increase further when the Fed eases.

## 3. What may have changed since publication
- Their window is the announcement day. Mine is the 24 h before, exit 5 minutes before the news: a different window, with the same actors.

## 4. What I take from it for my own work
- The bridge from equities to FX, and the basket construction: short USD against several currencies rather than one pair.
- A condition to test: more uncertainty, more premium. I tested it: in my window the drift did **not** scale with uncertainty (box 2.6 of [[FOMC Drift]]). Theirs is a different window; I wrote down that mine did not behave like theirs.
